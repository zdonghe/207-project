# Team Contract

**Members:** Nicholas Cheng, Belina Zhu, Sophie Pigott, Dong He

## 1. Purpose of this Contract

This contract sets out shared expectations and commitments for how our team
will collaborate during the course. It is designed to promote accountability,
professionalism, and mutual support as we learn, complete in-class activities,
and develop our course project together. It also gives us a shared basis for
peer and self-evaluations, so those assessments reflect agreed, observable
criteria.

This contract is binding for the duration of the course and may be revised only
with the agreement of all team members.

## 2. Team Norms and Expectations

### a) Communication

We will use an Instagram group chat as our primary communication channel. Team
members will respond to messages within 24 hours on weekdays and 48 hours on
weekends. A reaction or one-line acknowledgement counts as a response.

If a member will be unavailable for more than 48 hours, they will notify the
team in advance and hand off anything that would otherwise block someone else.

All communication will remain respectful, professional, and constructive.

### b) Attendance and Participation

Members will attend all scheduled classes and team meetings unless illness or
emergencies occur. If unable to attend, a member must notify the team in advance
and provide input asynchronously, including taking on a task so they still
contribute that week.

Each lab begins with a short stand-up: what we finished, what we are doing next,
and what is blocking. During team activities, every member will contribute
ideas and engage in the discussion.

Every member will speak during the final presentation and be able to explain the
design decisions behind the part they present.

### c) Decision Making

Decisions will be made by consensus when possible. If consensus cannot be
reached, a vote will be taken and the majority rules. If a vote ties, we gather
more information and revisit.

Technical decisions are recorded briefly in the relevant issue or a note in the
repository, so any member can explain and defend them at the presentation.

Changes to MVP scope or the user stories require team agreement and a
corresponding README update in the same pull request.

### d) Work Quality

All deliverables should be completed on time, tested, and meet the agreed
quality standard. If a member will miss a sub-deadline, they communicate at
least 24 hours before it with a new estimate.

Each pull request will be reviewed by at least two team members. A reviewer
should be someone other than the author, and if the change touches another
member's use case, that member must be one of the reviewers.

Before requesting review, the author confirms the branch compiles, the project's
test suite passes, and Checkstyle reports no new errors. Pull requests are kept
small, do one thing, link their issue, and describe the behaviour change; UI changes
include a screenshot.

Merged work passes the project's test suite and Checkstyle, includes interactor
tests for new use cases, and has no dependency-rule violations flagged in
review.

Members agree to review each other's work constructively and within 24 hours of
being requested during the week.

### e) Use Case Ownership

Each member takes the lead on at least one MVP use case, meaning they write the
majority of its interactor and related interfaces, controller, presenter, the UI
that triggers it, and the UI updates after it. They also coordinate with anyone
else whose code the use case touches. No member takes on another member's
assigned use case without discussing it first. Help comes as review, pairing, or
PR comments.

## 3. Conflict Resolution

If conflict arises:

- The team will first attempt to resolve the issue privately through open
  discussion, assuming good intent.
- If the issue persists for 48 hours, it will be raised at a team meeting.
- If it is still unresolved, the team will involve a member of the course
  teaching team as mediator.

Disagreements about project direction are resolved using the decision-making
process in section 2(c).

## 4. Accountability and Peer Evaluation

We will evaluate each other on these criteria, each of which is observable,
measurable, and specific:

1. Agreed work is delivered by the agreed date, or notice is given at least
   24 hours ahead with a new estimate.
2. Pull requests are reviewed within 24 hours, and labs and messages are
   answered within the agreed window.
3. Merged work passes the project's test suite and Checkstyle, includes
   interactor tests for new use cases, and has no dependency-rule violations
   flagged in review.
4. Each member's merged PRs implement a substantial part of their own use case,
   not just documentation or formatting.

Persistent non-participation or failure to meet expectations may result in lower
peer evaluation scores. All members agree to provide honest and fair peer
evaluations.
